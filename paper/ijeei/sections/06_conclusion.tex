\section{Conclusion}

This paper formulated data synchronization in a CQRS architecture as a discrete-time linear MIMO control problem and proposed an LQR-based controller that simultaneously regulates the message backlog and the read-database resource pressure by adjusting the batch size and poll interval. The state-space model is identified from open-loop measurements, and the LQR gain is derived offline from a quadratic cost that makes the trade-off between consistency and resource usage explicit through $Q$ and $R$.

Across five synthetic load patterns and three weighting configurations, the proposed controller achieves the lowest mean and max normalized regret compared to static, rule-based, and PID baselines, suggesting that an explicit model-based approach is both more accurate and more robust to changes in operational priorities than threshold heuristics.

Future work will extend the framework along three directions: (i) supporting multiple read replicas and multiple consumer topics, which introduces discrete switching dynamics not covered by the present linear model; (ii) online identification so that $A$ and $B$ track slow drifts in the system; and (iii) replacing the linear model with a piecewise-linear or learned dynamics to widen the validity region of the controller.
